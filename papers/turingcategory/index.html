Every headline statement is checked by Audit.lean to depend only on propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound; there are no sorrys and no axiom declarations. The development is relative to two interfaces:

  • TuringModel (Proposition 2.4, parts (1)–(4) and (6), the computability axioms) — discharged: the package model/ builds a concrete instance StableModel.mathlibModel from Mathlib’s Nat.Partrec.Code, with the same three Lean axioms. So the computability claims derived from it are theorems about the standard model of partial recursive functions. Part (5) of Proposition 2.4 is the interface Language, on which Arith is built; its fields are assumed.
  • Arith (the classical facts about first order arithmetic used by the paper: the Σ⁰ₙ/Π⁰ₙ hierarchy and its instance maps, decidability of Δ₀ truth, Q deciding Δ₀ sentences, and the representability of computable relations by arithmetic sentences, Lemma 4.2) — assumed, exactly as the paper assumes them (citing Tarski–Mostowski–Robinson and Hájek–Pudlák). The full list is in “Trusted base” below.

The steps of the paper that are not theorems about these interfaces are its assertions of provability in ZFC, first of all equation (5.12), the formalization inside ZFC of Proposition 5.5 (3). Equation (5.12) and the equivalence of Lemma 5.16 enter the second incompleteness theorems as explicit hypotheses (see “Second incompleteness”).

Exceptions, in the terms of the footnote of the manuscript’s Introduction. The standard facts taken as axioms are the fields of the structures Language, Arith, UndefData and UndefZData, listed in “Trusted base”, and the computability of the sets 𝓑ₙ (point 1 of “The restructured manuscript”). The standard interpretation i_{𝓐,𝓩} of Example 2.8 is an arbitrary translation iAZ: that it is a translation is not proved here. The assertions of provability in ZFC taken as hypotheses are listed in “Second incompleteness”. Accordingly the sentences Con₁ and Sndₙ (Lemma 5.9, Definition 5.10, and their forms at level n) are not constructed: the second incompleteness theorems are proved for an arbitrary sentence in their place, subject to these hypotheses. The remarks and examples which are not formalized: Remark 2.5 (𝒮 is a Turing category); Remark 2.7 (interpretations and guards); Example 3.2, Remark 3.3 and Example 3.19 (the halting and Diophantine examples); the comparison with experimental logics after Corollary 3.7; the notes on re-indexing and dovetailing in Remark 3.18; Remarks 5.14, 5.22 and 5.24; the first paragraph of Remark 5.25; and Corollary A.3. Remarks 5.15 and 6.10 state an expectation and facts from the literature. Of Proposition 2.4, the computability of Halt in part (2), used only in Example 3.2, and part (4)(c) are not formalized. The last claim of Theorem 1.5, that 𝒢 and a machine computing it are given explicitly, is not formalized: first_incompleteness_arith is an existence statement, and G₀ is defined through Classical.choose. The other assertions of provability in ZFC (the paragraph “Fixed choices”, Lemma 4.2, and their forms in Section 6.3) are not hypotheses of a theorem here, and have no counterpart. Beyond these, every statement depends only on the three axioms of Lean named above.

Four points.

  1. 𝓑ₙ is computable: a hypothesis. The paper uses that 𝓑₂ (resp. 𝓑ₙ₊₁) is a computable subset of 𝓐. The interface Arith has no syntax from which to prove this. So Lemma 5.19 and Corollaries 5.20, 5.21 are proved for any computable P ⊇ 𝓑ₙ, with R ∩ (P × 𝓛) a Σ⁰ₙ set. The paper’s statements are the case P = 𝓑ₙ, under the explicit hypothesis CompSet M (A.BoolComb n) (shadow_two, corollary_readingfirst, corollary_readingsecond and their level-n forms).
  2. Equation (5.12) is still a hypothesis (hcon, hsnd), stated for an arbitrary sentence con ∈ 𝓩, exactly as in Stable/Second.lean. The standard interpretation i_{𝓐,𝓩} enters as an arbitrary Translation A.toLanguage Z.
  3. The simplest case has a direct proof. If F ⊢ Con₁ (T), then by (5.12) ZFC proves the translation of c := ¬(Con₁ (T) ∧ ¬𝒢 (T)), so F ⊢ c and hence F ⊢ 𝒢 (T). The hypothesis “F ⊢ a whenever ZFC ⊢ a*” is used for this one sentence. The manuscript deduces the case from Theorem 5.11 through the theory H; both proofs are valid.
  4. The earlier modules keep the form for translations. Stable/Incompleteness.lean, Stable/Second.lean and Stable/NIncompleteness.lean state the theorems relative to a translation i. In the present manuscript that form is Example 5.23, read with the notation of Appendix B. The new statements are their case 𝓛 = 𝓐, i = id (Translation.id). Not formalized: Remark 5.24.

Building and checking

Lean 4.35.0 (pinned by lean-toolchain), no dependencies: core Lean only, no Mathlib, no Batteries. Five commands, the first of which installs Lean’s version manager elan:

curl -sSfL https://elan.lean-lang.org/elan-init.sh | sh -s -- -y
curl -LO https://yashamon.github.io/web2/papers/turingcategory/stablelean-0.4.9.zip
unzip stablelean-0.4.9.zip && cd stablelean
lake build                 # the whole library, kernel-checked (about a minute)
lake env lean Audit.lean   # the axioms of the 65 headline statements

On Windows, tar -xf stablelean-0.4.9.zip unpacks the archive. The build prints no warning. The audit prints 65 lines, one for each headline statement, all of the form

'Stable.stable_halting' depends on axioms: [propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]

So no headline statement depends on sorryAx (an incomplete proof), on an axiom declared by this project (there is none), or on Lean.ofReduceBool (trust in the compiler). The workflow .github/workflows/ci.yml, for a git hosting of the same files, runs the build and counts the 65 lines.

model/ is a separate Lake project depending on Mathlib (v4.35.0) and on this library (path ..). Its build prints the axioms of StableModel.mathlibModel:

cd model && lake exe cache get && lake build

How the paper’s objects are modelled

  • A partial map f : A → B is f : A → Option B; none is “undefined”. {±} is Bool, + = true.
  • M : ℕ → 𝓛 × {±} is ℕ → Option (L.Sent × Bool); a decision map D : B × ℕ → {±} is B → ℕ → Option Bool.
  • The map T' of 𝒯_B (𝒟_B) is defined for every T as e⁻¹ ∘ T (e⁻¹ ∘ T ∘ e), and T ∈ 𝒯_B (InTB) is the statement that T computes it. Consequently the lemmas stated in the paper for T ∈ 𝒯_B (Lemmas 3.16 and 3.22, Theorem 3.25 (3), Proposition 5.5, Theorems 6.8 and 6.9, …) are proved here for every T, with T^s = Stab (seqOf T); for T ∈ 𝒯_B this is the printed statement. T “stably computes M” (StablyComputes) is T ∈ 𝒯_B ∧ (T')^s = M^s, as printed.
  • Tuples of witnesses n̄ ∈ ℕ^r are contracted to a single number via the fixed surjections β_r of Section 2.2, bijective for r ≥ 1 (as the paper itself does for Tr_{Π₁}): for σ = ∃x̄ φ, instS σ v = φ(β_r v), and for π = ∀x̄ φ, instP π v = φ(β_r v). So 1-consistency reads: for every Σ⁰₁ sentence σ with F ⊢ⁱ σ there is v with F ⊬ⁱ ¬ instS σ v (Definitions 2.9 and B.6 with n̄ = β_r v). The decomposition σ = ∃x̄ φ is the one chosen by instS (the maximal leading block, in the standard model).
  • Level n of Section 6 is indexed by m = n - 1 (the number of extra variables): NDec m is “(m+1)-decided”, NStable m is “(m+1)-stable”, NSR m is a Σ⁰_{m+2} condition. ℕ^{m+1} is NB ℕ m, where the paper’s tuple (u₁, …, u_{m+1}) is ((u_{m+1}, u₁), u₂, …, u_m): the last variable is the base and u₁ is next to it; the slice M (u₁, ·) of Definition 6.1 is nslice m M u₁ (insert u₁ next to the base). Definition 6.1’s identification of an n-decision map on B with an (n-1)-decision map on B × ℕ is the definitional equality NB B (m+1) = NB (B × ℕ) m, so no coercions appear.
  • Lemma 4.2 is used only through: σ (T) ∈ Σ⁰₂ (resp. Σ⁰_{m+2}), computable in T, with ℕ ⊨ σ (T) iff T is Ω T-decided. The explicit shape ∃ u ∀ v̄ γ (u, v̄) with γ ∈ 𝓕₀ is not needed: Proposition 5.5 (2) only uses the Π⁰₁ instances instS (σ T) u and Q’s decision of their Δ₀ instances.
  • A Σ⁰ₙ₊₁ subset of an encoded set is modelled by the form with a computable relation given in Section 2.2 (IsSigma2Set, IsSigmaNSet). Its equivalence with definability by a Σ⁰ₙ₊₁ formula is a standard fact and is not formalized: Arith has no formulas with free variables.
  • Here ∨ is a defined connective (Stable/PropCalc.lean); the manuscript counts it among the logical symbols (Section 2.2). So translations are only required to preserve ¬, ∧ and derivability. Where a translation is a hypothesis this loses nothing. Where it is constructed, in the two existence statements of Remark 5.25 (exists_undefinable, exists_undefinable_ZFC), the statement is verified for maps preserving ¬ and ∧. The written proof in doc/undefinable.pdf extends the maps over all connectives.

Second incompleteness: what is and is not formalized

In its present form Theorem 5.11 is second_incompleteness_H (Stable/Plain.lean): for iAZ : 𝓐 → 𝓩, F = T^s 1-consistent, H ⊂ 𝓩 deductively closed and containing ZFC, with F ⊢ b for every b ∈ 𝓑₂ with iAZ b ∈ H, and any sentence con ∈ 𝓩 such that ZFC ∪ {con} ⊢ iAZ (𝒢 (T)), we have con ∉ H. Applied to con = Con₁ (T)*, the hypothesis is equation (5.12). Corollary 5.13, the simplest case, Corollary 5.21 and Theorem 6.9 are proved in the same way. Theorem A.2 (penrose_family) is deduced from Corollary 5.21, as in the manuscript. In it the sentence of axiom (3) is an arbitrary sentence pen of 𝓩, and ε is an arbitrary sentence in place of ε_{T₀}. The claims of ZFC-provability in its proof are one hypothesis: ZFC ∪ {ε, pen} ⊢ con, with the same arbitrary con. In the manuscript these are two steps: the sentence of axiom (3) implies its instance for T₀, and ε together with this instance implies Con₁ (T)*. The earlier form, for a family with one member, is penrose_two_way_delta. In it the sentence of axiom (3) is ε → conδ, for arbitrary sentences ε and conδ in place of ε_{T₀} and Con₁ (δ + ε_{T₀}). The two claims of ZFC-provability in its proof are hypotheses: ZFC ∪ {ε} ⊢ enum and, as in Corollary 5.13, ZFC ∪ {enum, conδ} ⊢ con, with enum in place of Enum (Sh_R (T₀), δ + ε_{T₀}) and the same arbitrary con. What follows describes the earlier form, for a translation; the remarks on (5.12) apply to both.

Theorem 1.1 (second_incompleteness_sigma) is stated for a Σ⁰₂ set F of sentences (IsSigma2Set F), not for a formula σ: the interface Arith has no syntax of formulas. It says that some machine presents F, by Corollary 3.7, and that the conclusion holds for every machine T with T^s = F. There are two hypotheses of provability in ZFC, for arbitrary sentences con and conσ: ZFC ∪ {con} ⊢ 𝒢 (T)* and ZFC ∪ {conσ} ⊢ con. For the machine T = T_σ of Lemma 5.16, with con = Con₁ (T_σ)* and conσ = Con₁ (σ)*, the first is (5.12) and the second follows from Lemma 5.16. Corollary 5.17 (second_incompleteness_sigma_simple) is treated in the same way. So are Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 5.17 at level n (n_second_incompleteness_sigma, n_second_incompleteness_sigma_simple), for a Σ⁰ₙ₊₁ set F (IsSigmaNSet m F, n = m + 1) and every machine T with T^{s_n} = F, with Sndₙ in place of Con₁. Not formalized: Remark 5.22.

For a translation, Theorem 5.11 is proved in the form (second_incompleteness): for 𝓩, iAZ : 𝓐 → 𝓩, i : 𝓩 → 𝓛 with j = i ∘ iAZ a 2-translation, F = T^s, F ⊢ⁱ ZFC, F 1-consistent relative to j, and any sentence con ∈ 𝓩 such that ZFC ∪ {con} ⊢ iAZ (𝒢 (T)), we have F ⊬ⁱ con. Applied to con = Con₁ (T, i), the hypothesis is equation (5.12), “ZFC ⊢ Con₁ (T, i) → i_{𝓐,𝓩} (𝒢 (T))”, the formalization of Proposition 5.5 (3) inside ZFC. Verifying (5.12) would require formalizing ZFC-provability and replaying the development inside it; this is outside the scope of the project, and the sentence Con₁ (T, i) itself (Lemma 5.9 and Definition 5.10 in their form before 11 November 2026) is not constructed. Corollary 5.13, Theorem 6.9 and the earlier form of Theorem A.2 (penrose) are proved in the same form, with “ZFC ⊢ Enum (T, δ) → (Con₁ (δ, i) ↔︎ Con₁ (T, i))” as a further hypothesis. Everything else in the proofs of these results is a theorem here.
